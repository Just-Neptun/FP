\section{Investigating a Single Neuron}

\subsection{A Continuously Spiking Neuron}

We investigate the \texttt{Brainscales-2} System with a single neuron.

First, we will choose parameters such that the neuron spikes continuously without external input. The resulting membrane potential over time is given in \autoref{fig:single_neuron_continuous_spike}. The configured parameters are also given below.

\begin{gather}
    \text{\texttt{v\_leak}} = 580 \\
    \text{\texttt{v\_threshold}} = 220 \\
    \text{\texttt{v\_reset}} = 370 \\
    \text{\texttt{i\_bias\_leak}} = 100
\end{gather}

\begin{figure}[htbp]

    \caption{A Single Neuron, Parameters Set So It Continuously Spikes}

\end{figure}


\subsection{Simple Spike Train Statistics}

Once these parameters were determined, we analyzed the neuron's average firing rate, otherwise known as its frequency. This was done by calculating the times between each spike and then averaging them. The average firing rate was \qty{36.8 \pm 0.3}{\hertz}.

\subsection{Recording the f-I curve of a silicon neuron}

Next, we want to study the behavior of a single neuron stimulated with a current source. For this, we introduce a new parameter for our neuron model. This will allow us to enable/disable a constant current source and observe the impact.

We varied the constant current source between \num{0} and \num{800} and calculated the frequency of firing for each trial. The resulting firing rates were plotted against current in \autoref{fig:f-I_curve_freq_vs_I}. Standard deviations were calculated within each trial to obtain an error. While are present in the graph, the uncertainties are very small and are therefore not easily visible.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.7\textwidth]{./02_Single_Neuron/Graphs/fp_task1_freq_vs_I.png}
    \caption{Dependence of Firing Rate of Neuron on Constant Current Source}
    \label{fig:f-I_curve_freq_vs_I}
\end{figure}

As is visible in the graph, increasing the current applied to the neuron increases its spiking frequency. This makes sense because a higher current will charge the capacitance of the neuron faster. This causes \( V_\mathrm{mem} \) to also increase faster, meaning the threshold voltage is arrived sooner after each spike.

The relationship seems to be somewhat linear, but more examination might be required to rule out a more logarithmic relationship. This depends on the importance of the seemingly shallower slope at the \num{800} current end of the scale relative to the low currents.


\subsection{Synaptic Stimuli and PSP Stacking}
